# AI Process Optimization: Fix the Process, Then Automate It

Automating a process that already has too many steps, handoffs and workarounds tends to produce a faster version of the same mess. Clickmasters approaches AI process optimization the other way round: we map how the work really runs, take out what does not need to exist, decide where AI and people fit, and only then automate. The goal is a simpler process first and a faster one second.

## What AI process optimization is

AI process optimization is the analysis and redesign of a specific business process before automating it. It maps how work actually flows, finds handoffs, rework and waiting time, removes unnecessary steps, and defines a to-be process that shows where rules, AI and human approvals belong, ready to be automated.

It is one of [the full range of AI automation options](/products/general-ai-automation) we offer, and it often begins with [the free automation audit](/free-automation-audit), which points to the processes worth looking at first.

## Why automating a messy process makes it worse

Most processes were never designed. They grew. A step was added after one mistake, a spreadsheet appeared to cover a gap in the CRM, and an approval was introduced for a manager who left years ago. When that process is automated as it stands:

- **Workarounds get locked in.** The automation copies the spreadsheet step instead of asking why the spreadsheet exists.
- **Rework speeds up.** If information is often wrong at the start, automation simply moves the wrong information faster.
- **Exceptions multiply.** An unclear process produces many unclear cases, and each one ends up in someone's inbox.
- **The build gets bigger than it needs to be.** Every unnecessary step becomes another thing to build, test and maintain.

Removing a step is usually cheaper than automating it.

## How we analyze a process

We do not use process-mining software. The work is hands-on and based on four sources.

### Interviews with the people who do the work

We talk to the people who run the process day to day, plus the people who receive its output. They know where things get stuck, which steps they skip and which ones they repeat.

### Walkthroughs of real cases

We follow actual recent cases from start to finish, on screen, in the systems your team uses. Real cases reveal the detours that the official process description leaves out.

### System data

Where your systems allow, we look at exports, timestamps, status histories and inbox patterns to see where work waits and where it bounces back.

### Mapping the as-is process

We bring this together in a map of the current process: each step, who does it, which system it touches, where it waits and where it loops back.

## What we look for

- **Handoffs:** each time work passes between people or teams, information can be lost and work can wait.
- **Rework:** steps that are repeated because something was missing or wrong earlier.
- **Waiting time:** points where nothing happens until someone gets to it.
- **Duplicate entry:** the same details typed into more than one system.
- **Approvals that add nothing:** sign-offs that are always granted or that duplicate another check.
- **Unclear ownership:** steps where nobody is quite sure who is responsible.
- **Judgment points:** decisions that genuinely need a person and should stay with one.

## Example: client onboarding, as-is and to-be

A typical example from a service business:

| Step | As-is process | To-be process |
|---|---|---|
| Handover from sales | Rep emails operations a summary; details are often missing | Signed deal triggers a structured intake form tied to the CRM record |
| Data entry | Operations re-types client details into the project tool and a spreadsheet | Details flow from the CRM into the project tool; spreadsheet removed |
| Document collection | Staff email requests and chase replies by hand | Automated request with reminders; AI checks returned documents for completeness |
| Review | Two managers approve the same file separately | One named approver signs off, with the checklist pre-filled |
| Kickoff | Coordinator schedules by email back-and-forth | Client books from available slots; team is notified |
| Status updates | Client emails to ask for progress | Updates sent at agreed milestones |

In this example, a spreadsheet and a duplicate approval disappear before any automation is built. What remains is shorter, has one clear approval point and is easier to automate reliably.

## From redesign to automation

Once the to-be process is agreed, each remaining step gets a label: handled by fixed rules, handled by AI (reading, classifying, drafting), or handled by a person. That split follows the approach we describe in [splitting the work between rules, AI and people](/products/general-ai-automation/intelligent-business-automation). For processes that run across departments and mostly follow clear rules, the next step is [automating the redesigned operational process](/services/business-process-automation).

Not every improvement needs automation. Some changes are simply new rules, a shared form or a removed step. We point those out as well.

## How this differs from AI strategy

Process optimization works on one process at a time: how it runs, what to remove, what to automate. It answers "how should this process work?"

Company-level questions are a different engagement. If you need to decide where AI fits across the business, build a roadmap or set priorities across departments, that is [AI strategy and advisory](/services/ai-strategy-advisory). The two fit together, but they are not the same thing.

## How the work runs

Optimization sits inside our published process, mainly in the first two steps:

1. **Discovery.** Interviews, walkthroughs and system data on the chosen process.
2. **Strategy and mapping.** The as-is map, the issues found and the to-be design, with triggers, decision points, actions, integrations and approval points. You approve the plan before anything is built.
3. **Development.** The redesigned process is automated in your systems.
4. **Testing.** Real cases run through it, including edge cases and failure scenarios.
5. **Deployment.** It goes live, with documentation and access handed over.
6. **Monitoring and improvement.** Monitoring and improvement after launch is part of our process; scope and terms are agreed per project.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is AI process optimization?
It is the analysis and redesign of a business process before it is automated. We map how the work runs today, remove unnecessary steps and define where rules, AI and people should each take over. The result is a simpler process that is ready to automate.

### Should you optimize a process before automating it?
Usually, yes. Automating a process as it stands tends to lock in workarounds and duplicate steps. A short redesign first often means a smaller, more reliable automation.

### How do you find bottlenecks in a business process?
We interview the people who do the work, walk through real recent cases and look at system data such as timestamps and status histories. Then we map the process and mark handoffs, rework and waiting time. Bottlenecks tend to show up where work waits for a person or loops back.

### What makes a process a good candidate for AI?
A good candidate is repetitive, happens often and involves reading, sorting or drafting from varied input. If a task follows rules, plain automation may be enough. Steps that need real judgment should stay with people, with AI preparing the information.

### How is process optimization different from AI strategy?
Process optimization looks at one process in depth and redesigns it. AI strategy works at company level, deciding where AI fits across the business and in what order. Many clients start with one and move to the other.

### Do you use process-mining software?
No. Our analysis is based on interviews, walkthroughs, system data and mapping. That suits most growing businesses, where the most useful information sits with the people doing the work.

### What do we receive at the end?
You receive an as-is map, a list of the issues found and a to-be process design showing approval points and what should be automated. You approve the plan before anything is built. You can then build it with us or stop there.
