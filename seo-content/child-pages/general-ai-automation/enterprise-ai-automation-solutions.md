# Enterprise AI Automation Solutions with Governance Built In

In a large organization, building one automation is rarely the hard part. The hard part is deciding which of dozens of requests to fund, keeping every workflow secure and traceable, and rolling changes out without disrupting teams. Enterprise AI automation treats that work as a program, not a string of one-off projects. We help you prioritize, govern and deliver automations across departments, running in your own cloud and accounts, with people approving the decisions that matter. For smaller-scale starting points, see our guide to [AI automation for business, from first workflow to company-wide](/products/general-ai-automation).

## What enterprise AI automation means

Enterprise AI automation is the coordinated use of workflow automation, AI models and system integrations across multiple departments, managed as a portfolio with shared governance. It covers how automations are chosen, built, secured, approved, logged and changed, not only what each individual workflow does.

## Why automation stalls in large organizations

- **Shadow automations multiply.** Teams build their own workflows on different platforms, and nobody has a full list of what runs where.
- **Security reviews arrive late.** A promising pilot stops when IT or security sees it for the first time.
- **Priorities are unclear.** Requests come from every department, and the loudest one often wins.
- **Change is risky.** Updating a workflow that touches the ERP or customer data has no clear approval path.
- **Knowledge sits with individuals.** When the builder moves on, documentation is thin or missing.
- **AI adds new questions.** Leaders need to know where data goes, which model processes it and who reviews the output.

## The program view: from scattered projects to a managed portfolio

### Portfolio prioritization
We gather candidate processes from each department and assess them on business value, effort, risk, data sensitivity and dependencies. The result is a ranked backlog and a roadmap your leadership can approve. Company-level direction and operating model questions belong with [our AI strategy advisory service](/services/ai-strategy-advisory), while individual processes that need redesign first go through [process-level optimization](/products/general-ai-automation/ai-process-optimization) before they are automated.

### Governance controls

| Control | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Access | Service accounts with least-privilege permissions, managed in your identity and access setup |
| Logging | A record of each run, its inputs, AI outputs and actions taken |
| Approvals | Defined points where a named role must approve before the workflow acts |
| Change control | Versioned workflows, documented changes and agreed sign-off before updates go live |
| Ownership | Every automation has a named business owner and technical documentation |
| Visibility | An inventory of automations and regular status updates for stakeholders |

Status and exception data can feed dashboards through [reporting automation for leadership](/services/ai-reporting-automation), so sponsors see what is running without asking for manual updates.

### Security review cooperation
We work within your security and procurement process rather than around it. That means documenting data flows, the systems and AI providers involved, where processing happens and how access is controlled, then answering your reviewers' questions. We do not claim certifications. Formal compliance with any framework your organization follows is a shared responsibility that depends on your specific setup and is confirmed for your project.

### Phased rollout
Automations go live with a pilot group first, then expand once the owners are satisfied. Each phase has its own sign-off, so a problem in one area does not hold up the whole program.

## How it works: one cross-department workflow under governance

Take a purchase request process that spans operations, finance and the ERP:

1. **Request:** an employee submits a purchase request through a form or Microsoft Teams.
2. **Enrichment:** the workflow pulls supplier and budget data from the ERP and checks for an existing contract.
3. **AI review:** an AI step reads the request and any attached quote, summarizes it and flags missing information or policy questions.
4. **Approval:** the summary goes to the budget holder in Teams. Requests above defined thresholds also route to finance. Each approver sees the original documents alongside the summary.
5. **System update:** once approved, the workflow creates the purchase record in the ERP through its API.
6. **Logging:** every step, approval and AI output is logged, and the requester is notified.
7. **Exceptions:** unclear or rejected requests return to a person with full context instead of being dropped.

The approval rules, logging fields and access permissions are agreed and documented before the workflow is built.

## Benefits of a governed program

- **Clear priorities,** so effort goes to the processes that matter most to the business.
- **Fewer surprises in security review,** because documentation exists from the start.
- **Traceable activity,** with logs that show what each automation did and who approved it.
- **Safer changes,** through version control and agreed sign-off.
- **Lower dependency on individuals,** since every workflow has an owner and documentation.
- **Steady expansion,** with phased rollouts that build confidence across departments.

## Technologies and integrations

Enterprise programs typically involve ERPs such as SAP, NetSuite or Oracle; CRMs such as Salesforce or HubSpot; Microsoft Teams, Outlook and Slack; support platforms such as Zendesk; SQL databases and cloud data platforms; and AWS, Azure or GCP for hosting. Orchestration can run on n8n (including self-hosted), Make or custom code, with AI models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or open-source providers. Agent work may use LangChain or LlamaIndex, and connections use REST APIs, webhooks and MCP connectors. Every system connection depends on the system offering an API or integration, confirmed during discovery. The [integration layer between AI and your systems](/products/general-ai-automation/ai-integration-services) is often the largest piece of enterprise work.

## Implementation process

1. **Discovery:** stakeholder interviews, system inventory, candidate processes and constraints from IT and security.
2. **Strategy and mapping:** portfolio prioritization, then detailed mapping of triggers, decision points, actions, integrations and approval points for the first workflows. Your sponsors approve the plan before anything is built.
3. **Development:** builds in your environment, following your access and change policies.
4. **Testing:** testing with realistic data, including edge cases, failure scenarios and permission checks, with your owners involved.
5. **Deployment:** phased go-live with documentation and access handed over to your teams.
6. **Monitoring and improvement:** monitoring and improvement after launch is part of our process; scope and terms are agreed per project.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do enterprises scale AI automation?
Enterprises scale AI automation by managing it as a portfolio: ranking candidate processes, applying shared governance and rolling out in phases. Each automation gets a named owner, documentation and logging, so the program can grow without losing control.

### How do you govern AI automations across departments?
We use agreed controls for access, logging, approvals and change control, plus an inventory of every automation and its owner. These rules are defined during strategy and mapping and documented before workflows go live.

### How do you handle security reviews?
We cooperate with your security review by documenting data flows, systems, AI providers, processing locations and access controls, and by answering your reviewers' questions. We do not claim certifications; formal compliance is a shared responsibility confirmed for your specific setup.

No, we do not claim certifications. We design with privacy and security in mind and work within your policies, and formal compliance depends on your specific setup and is confirmed for your project.

### How do you decide which automations to build first?
